# Spanish losses to Britain need assembling, not downloading

No free, ready-made dataset maps ships sunk, captured or destroyed by the Royal Navy, Spanish or otherwise, with coordinates. You can build one, though, from four kinds of material: an event-based warship reference (Three Decks, which has no export and is "all rights reserved"), structured but patchy Wikidata and Wikipedia records, British prize court records (the Prize Papers portal, whose export options could not be verified), and logbook positions (CLIWOC) that can pin some actions to real coordinates. Almost none of these sources carry latitude and longitude for a capture. Geocoding battle and port names is the central task, not a side step. One coded, ready subset turned up: the SlaveVoyages Trans-Atlantic database has FATE codes for "captured by British" alongside a ship flag field and departure and landing ports, which gives origin, destination and captor for Spanish-flagged slave ships, though only at port level. The other three questions are in better shape. Colonial extent has three free global or regional polygon sets that reach back to 1492. Wreck layers with coordinates are plentiful and openly licensed, but they rarely record flag or cause of loss. Route data exists mainly as daily logbook positions (CLIWOC, NOAA's East India Company logs), plus one true route shapefile limited to England and Wales. Trade data is mostly tabular flows keyed to place names. Every source below carries its page date and a tier: T1 is the data's publisher or owner, T2 is an established secondary or reference source, and T3 is a community re-upload or other anecdotal source.

## No dataset records British captures of Spanish ships with coordinates

The richest single source for the goal is **Three Decks**. It reports about **30,984 ships and 1,089 actions**, and it carries the notice "all rights reserved" (undated, T2) ([Three Decks](https://threedecks.org/)). Its data model stores captures, wrecks, burnings, renamings and battles as dated events. Each ownership period is a separate record, so a Spanish ship taken into British service appears as a Spanish record followed by a British one (undated, T2) ([Three Decks Data Definitions](https://threedecks.org/index.php?display_type=sitedata)). That is exactly the structure a timelapse needs. The San Ildefonso page, for example, records capture at Trafalgar on 21.10.1805 and arrival at Gibraltar under tow, but it gives **place names only, with no coordinates and no download** (undated, T2) ([San Ildefonso](https://threedecks.org/index.php?display_type=show_ship&id=2682)). The terms URL shows only the copyright line, with no clause on scraping or bulk export (undated) ([Three Decks terms URL](https://threedecks.org/index.php?display_type=show_terms)). The research notes read this as no implied permission to scrape and suggest asking the site's owner, Cy Harrison, by email.

Wikidata offers a legal bridge into Three Decks. A QLever count run on 2026-10-02 found **2,042 Wikidata items carrying a Three Decks ship ID (P11085)** (T3, community-edited) ([QLever P11085 count](https://qlever.dev/api/wikidata?query=SELECT+%28COUNT%28%3Fs%29+AS+%3Fn%29+WHERE+%7B+%3Fs+%3Chttp%3A%2F%2Fwww.wikidata.org%2Fprop%2Fdirect%2FP11085%3E+%3Fid+%7D)). The property "destroyed by" (P1028) appears on 17,293 items, but that count covers every kind of subject, not only ships (2026-10-02, T3) ([QLever P1028 count](https://qlever.dev/api/wikidata?query=SELECT+%28COUNT%28%3Fs%29+AS+%3Fn%29+WHERE+%7B+%3Fs+%3Chttp%3A%2F%2Fwww.wikidata.org%2Fprop%2Fdirect%2FP1028%3E+%3Fid+%7D)). The test that matters most could not be run: how many Spanish ship items carry coordinates, a capture event or "destroyed by". Every join query failed through the research proxy, so this needs re-running from an unrestricted machine. The property page for P11085 itself was seen only in search results and was not opened ([Wikidata P11085](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P11085)).

English Wikipedia gives semi-structured text. "List of ships of the line of Spain" covers 1640 to 1854, with launch place and a fate sentence for each ship, such as San Juan Nepomuceno, "captured by Britain at the Battle of Trafalgar". It has no coordinates (undated, T3, community-edited) ([List of ships of the line of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ships_of_the_line_of_Spain)). "List of naval battles" has Date, Battle, Location, Winners and Losers columns, also without coordinates (undated, T3) ([List of naval battles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_naval_battles)). The yearly shipwreck lists are the only Wikipedia source with any per-loss coordinates, and those are sparse. The 1797 list, for instance, records Arrogante "burnt at Trinidad to prevent capture by the Royal Navy" (undated, T3) ([List of shipwrecks in 1797](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_shipwrecks_in_1797); [List of shipwrecks in the 1700s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_shipwrecks_in_the_1700s)). The Spanish Wikipedia annexes of ships of the line, frigates and wrecks surfaced only in search results and could not be opened (unverified) ([Anexo: Navíos de línea](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anexo:Nav%C3%ADos_de_l%C3%ADnea_de_la_Armada_Espa%C3%B1ola); [Anexo: Fragatas](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anexo:Fragatas_a_vela_de_la_Armada_Espa%C3%B1ola)).

For non-naval Spanish ships, the British prize courts are the primary record. The **Prize Papers** project covers papers from about **35,000 ships across 14 wars, 1652 to 1817**. Its portal launched with 55 HCA 45 case books on about **1,500 ships seized by Britain between 1793 and 1815**, searchable by ship, master, geographic area and date (undated, T1) ([TNA portal launch](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/about/news/prize-papers-research-portal-launched/); [Prize Papers](https://www.prizepapers.de/)). The portal returned HTTP 403, so its licence, bulk export, API and coverage of Spanish prizes are **unverified** ([Prize Papers portal](https://portal.prizepapers.de/index/)). Lloyd's List for 1741 to 1826 reports captures and losses, but only as a scanned facsimile with OCR text and no stated licence (uploaded 2009-07-21, T1 facsimile) ([Lloyd's List, Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/lloydslist17411800farn); [HathiTrust 1785-1786](https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=mdp.39015020212893), undated).

On the Spanish side, Todo a Babor has a curated list by Juan García of Spanish ships of the line and frigates lost between 1796 and 1801 and between 1804 and 1808. Each entry is classed as apresado (captured), destruido (destroyed) or incendiado (burnt), and names the battle. It has no coordinates and no stated licence (published 2005-05-17, modified 2023-02-10, T2) ([Todo a Babor loss list](https://www.todoababor.es/historia/navios-de-linea-y-fragatas-espanolas-perdidos-ademas-buques-apresados-extranjeros-agregados-a-real-armada/)). It also has a reverse list of British warships taken by Spain, 1718 to 1808 (published 2015-06-18, modified 2023-02-10, T2) ([Todo a Babor reverse list](https://www.todoababor.es/historia/buques-guerra-britanicos-apresados-espanoles/)). The Spanish Navy compiled a database of 1,580 wrecks from its archives, but the announcement says nothing about public access, coordinates or cause of loss (2013-02-01, T1) ([Ministerio de Defensa](https://www.defensa.gob.es/portaldecultura/-/1580-naufragios-documentados)).

The one coded subset is in SlaveVoyages. The 2019 Trans-Atlantic codebook assigns **FATE codes 10 to 13 to capture by the British** and 14 to 17 to capture by the Spanish. NATIONAL records the flag (2019 codebook, undated, T1) ([SPSS Codebook 2019](https://www.slavevoyages.org/static/uploads/SPSS_Codebook_2019.pdf)). Departure, purchase and landing ports are 5-digit place codes with no coordinates (codebook 2023-11-06, T1) ([SPSS Codebook 2023-11-06](https://legacy.slavevoyages.org/documents/download/SPSS_Codebook_2023-11-06.pdf)). Filtering FATE 10 to 13 with NATIONAL set to Spain gives a ready list of Spanish slave ships captured by Britain, with origin and intended destination. It covers slave voyages only.

## Wreck layers have coordinates but rarely flag, cause or voyage

Government hydrographic and heritage datasets supply most of the free wreck coordinates. They describe hazards and sites, not voyages. Only **Nova Scotia's Marine Heritage Shipwrecks Database** has structured voyage_from, voyage_to, cargo and cause_of_event columns, and its location field is text with no lat/long column (about 4,939 rows, updated 2024-07-05, T1) ([Open Data Nova Scotia](https://data.novascotia.ca/Arts-Culture-and-History/Marine-Heritage-Shipwrecks-Database/rq3a-h5hk); [Socrata column metadata](https://data.novascotia.ca/api/views/rq3a-h5hk.json)). Ireland's Wreck Inventory is the largest free CSV with documentary history. It has over 18,000 known and potential wreck sites under CC BY 4.0, but the file download was blocked during research, so its **column names are unverified** (created 2023-08-10, modified 2024-06-21, T1) ([data.gov.ie WIID](https://data.gov.ie/dataset/national-monuments-service-wreck-inventory-of-ireland)). Contrary to the brief's assumption that it would be paid, the UKHO wrecks set of over 94,000 records is **free under the Open Government Licence**; its file format is not stated (undated, T1) ([ADMIRALTY Marine data](https://www.admiralty.co.uk/access-data/marine-data)).

Several sources fall short of a usable download. Historic England's National Marine Heritage Record holds about 37,000 shipwrecks but has **no direct bulk download**; access is by request or through portals (undated, T1) ([Historic England NMHR](https://historicengland.org.uk/research/support-and-collaboration/heritage-information-access-simplified/national-marine-heritage-record/)). The Wales maritime dataset is said to be downloadable through DataMapWales (undated, T1) ([RCAHMW](https://rcahmw.gov.uk/ships-wrecks-and-documentary-sources/)), but the layer page returned a sign-in screen, so it is **registration-gated and unverified**. Wrecksite.eu needs a free subscription (214,790 wrecks, per a 2025-03-31 review, T2/T3) ([Global Maritime History](https://globalmaritimehistory.com/online-shipwreck-databases-for-maritime-historians-and-archaeologists/)). The Kaggle "Wrecks and Obstructions" upload could not be read (**unverified**, T3) ([Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/thedevastator/wrecks-and-obstructions-dataset)). Two open academic databases do have origin and destination fields, but both end at 1500 and so fall outside the Age of Sail: DARMC (status July 2021, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) ([DARMC](https://darmc.harvard.edu/data-availability)) and OxREP (undated, licence not stated) ([OxREP](https://oxrep.classics.ox.ac.uk/databases/shipwrecks_database/)).

Spanish galleon and treasure-fleet wrecks have **no free download**. The Ministerio de Cultura inventory of Spanish wrecks off the US and Bahamas is an interactive map only (undated, T1) ([cultura.gob.es](https://www.cultura.gob.es/cultura/areas/patrimonio/mc/patrimonio-subacuatico/actuaciones/naufragios-espanoles.html)). Its site shows 150 wrecks with port of origin, cargo and circumstances of loss, but no export (undated, T1) ([shipwrecks.es](https://shipwrecks.es/es/)). A 2019 press reprint describes a first inventory of 681 Spanish ships lost in the Americas between 1492 and 1898, with no confirmed release (2019-02, T2) ([patrimoniosubacuatico.net](https://patrimoniosubacuatico.net/mapa-del-tesoro/)). No wreck dataset found has a structured flag field for the Age of Sail except the paid ShipwreckMap. Separating losses to naval action from losses to weather therefore means text-classifying the AWOIS HISTORY field, the WIID history and the Nova Scotia description fields.

## Three free polygon sets trace colonization from 1492

For colonial extent, **Cliopatria** offers the best time resolution. It holds about 15,000 records on more than 1,600 polities, each with an inclusive FromYear to ToYear range (paper 2025-02-12, T1) ([Scientific Data](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04516-9)). It ships as a single GeoJSON under CC BY 4.0 (v0.2.0, T1) ([Cliopatria GitHub](https://github.com/Seshat-Global-History-Databank/cliopatria)). The researcher counted 4,168 of its 13,765 features overlapping 1492 to 1860. **historical-basemaps** adds a SUBJECTO field naming the colonial power, which suits colouring by colonizer, but it offers only 11 snapshots between 1492 and 1815 and has nothing between 1815 and 1878 ([index.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/aourednik/historical-basemaps/master/index.json)). It is GPL-3.0, and its README calls it a work in progress to check against other sources (last commit 2026-09-15, T1) ([historical-basemaps](https://github.com/aourednik/historical-basemaps)). For the Spanish theatre specifically, **HGIS de las Indias** has Spanish American jurisdictions, provinces and audiencias for seven snapshot years from 1701 to 1808, plus a territorial gazetteer with START and END fields (gazetteer v4.0 updated 2023-10-26, T1) ([DataCite YPEU5E](https://api.datacite.org/dois/10.7910/DVN/YPEU5E); [DataCite HZJGKA](https://api.datacite.org/dois/10.7910/DVN/HZJGKA)). Its Dataverse pages could not be opened, so the text of its "custom terms" is **unverified**; the homepage says non-commercial use (undated, T1) ([hgis-indias.net](https://www.hgis-indias.net/)).

## Routes exist as logbook points; trade exists as named places

The best Age of Sail route material is **CLIWOC 2.1**: daily noon positions from British, Dutch, French and Spanish logbooks, 1754-01-11 to 1855-07-29, published under CC BY 3.0 (2007-05-03, T1) ([PANGAEA 611088](https://doi.pangaea.de/10.1594/PANGAEA.611088); [PANGAEA 743343](https://doi.pangaea.de/10.1594/PANGAEA.743343), 2007-09-25). The most GIS-ready copy is Steven Ottens' **GeoPackage of 287,114 records** with decimal lat/lon added (undated, no licence stated, T3) ([stvno.github.io CLIWOC](https://stvno.github.io/page/cliwoc/)). Records carry ShipName, Nationality, VoyageFrom, VoyageTo and a voyage start date, so you can rebuild per-voyage tracks with origin and destination metadata. A 2019 analysis also lists **WarsAndFights and WarsAndFightsMemo** fields, which bear directly on the capture goal (2019-07-03, T3, anecdotal) ([Tekenlight blog](https://blog.tekenlight.com/2019/07/03/blog-ship-voyage.html)). NOAA's keyed English East India Company logbooks add about 893 logbooks with daily noon lat/lon in CSV, ASCII and XML (modified 2023-06-23, T1) ([NOAA NCEI C00785](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/metadata/landing-page/bin/iso?id=gov.noaa.ncdc:C00785)).

No authoritative global shapefile of Age of Sail routes was found. The only true route geometry from a primary publisher is the England and Wales coastal network, built mainly from Collins' 1693 sailing guide ([ReShare 853711](https://reshare.ukdataservice.ac.uk/853711/)), plus HGIS's generalized flotas y galeones lines. For the Carrera de Indias there is **no open voyage dataset**. Chaunu's *Séville et l'Atlantique* is search-only on HathiTrust (undated) ([HathiTrust](https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/000858324)), and the New Spain Fleets project shows no download yet (undated, T1) ([New Spain Fleets](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/newspainfleets/?page_id=20&lang=en)). Lloyd's Register 1764 to 1999 is free only as page scans (undated, T1) ([LR Foundation](https://heritage.lrfoundation.org.uk/lloyds-register-of-ships-online)). EIC ship records at eicships.threedecks.org are all rights reserved with no downloads (undated, T2) ([EIC Ships](https://eicships.threedecks.org/)).

Trade data is spatially thin. The ready-made point layers are HGIS de las Indias' **Ports of Spanish America, 1701-1808** as a zipped shapefile (2019-04-04, custom terms, T1) ([DataCite UXDJLQ](https://api.datacite.org/dois/10.7910/DVN/UXDJLQ)), its layers for consulados, royal treasuries, mints and shipyards ([DataCite listing](https://api.datacite.org/dois?query=creators.name:Stangl%20AND%20publisher:%22Harvard%20Dataverse%22&page[size]=40)), and the 479 England and Wales ports. TOFLIT18 has more than 500,000 French trade flows from 1716 to 1821 under ODbL, with no stated geocoding (2024-09-11, T1) ([Zenodo TOFLIT18](https://zenodo.org/records/13749501)). RICardo spans 1790 to 1938 under ODbL, but the researcher's count shows only 40 flow rows per decade before 1820, so it is usable mainly from the 1830s (last commit 2026-09-11, T1) ([RICardo GitHub](https://github.com/medialab/ricardo_data)). Federico-Tena is polity-level from 1800, with no licence stated (2018-05-08, T1) ([UC3M](https://www.uc3m.es/ss/Satellite/UC3MInstitucional/es/TextoMixta/1371246237481/Federico-Tena_World_Trade_Historical_Database)). British CUST 3 customs ledgers, 1696 to 1780, are **paid** subscription scans (undated, T1) ([British Online Archives](https://britishonlinearchives.com/collections/129/volumes/1492/imports-and-exports-1696-1780)). No free digitized Spanish Contratación or Almojarifazgo dataset was found.

Since most route and trade sources are keyed to place names, gazetteers do the geocoding. Usable ones include the World Historical Gazetteer sets: Old World Trade (4,117 places, modified 2025-10-15) ([WHG 12](https://whgazetteer.org/datasets/12/places)), Viabundus (10,393, modified 2025-09-10) ([WHG 1245](https://whgazetteer.org/datasets/1245/places)), the Atlas of Mutual Heritage VOC/WIC places (1,322, modified 2025-10-13) ([WHG 819](https://whgazetteer.org/datasets/819/places)) and African Ports 1887 (305, modified 2025-09-28) ([WHG 657](https://whgazetteer.org/datasets/657/places)). The WHG records list no licence for most source data; WHG's own additions are CC BY 4.0. Beyond WHG, there is the GLOBALISE VOC place set (2023-05, CC BY 4.0, T1) ([Zenodo 13341556](https://zenodo.org/records/13341556)), and a SlaveVoyages common-ports CSV with lat/long on Google Drive, referenced by a UCSC guide (guide 2026-08-13, T2). That CSV itself was **not opened** ([UCSC guide](https://guides.library.ucsc.edu/DS/Resources/Class-Specific/LALS194E/MappingData)).

## A four-layer build turns these sources into a Spanish-losses timelapse

The working plan is a **loss-event table** with one row per ship per fate event. It needs ship name, nation before the event, date, fate type (captured, destroyed, burnt, wrecked), British captor where known, place name, latitude and longitude, coordinate method, and origin and destination where known. Fate type must be its own field, because the sources split losses this way: Todo a Babor (apresado, destruido, incendiado), SlaveVoyages FATE codes, Three Decks events, and Canmore's wreck versus casualty classes all distinguish them. Four layers feed the table, each with a specific join key.

| Layer | Source | Join key | Geocoding need | Main gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Naval losses spine | Wikidata items with P11085; Wikipedia Spanish ships of the line list; Todo a Babor 1796-1808 | Three Decks ship ID (P11085); else ship name + launch year + fate date | Battle name or port to point; test whether battle items carry P625 | Wikidata coverage of fate and coordinates unmeasured; Three Decks reuse needs permission |
| Battle locations | List of naval battles; Wikidata battle items | Battle name + date | Location text to point | Location column has no coordinates |
| Merchant and slaver captures | Prize Papers (1793-1815 case books); SlaveVoyages FATE 10-13 with NATIONAL = Spain; Lloyd's List OCR | Ship name + master + date; SlaveVoyages VOYAGEID and 5-digit port codes | Port codes to the ports CSV, then WHG and HGIS ports for the rest | Prize Papers export unverified; Lloyd's needs manual extraction |
| Coordinate refinement | CLIWOC logbooks (British and Spanish), WarsAndFights fields | ShipName + date, fuzzy matched as in [exploringShipLogbooks](https://github.com/clarka34/exploringShipLogbooks) (undated, MIT, T3) | None; positions are native | Only about 10% of known logbooks, 1750 to 1855 |
| Backdrop | Cliopatria by year; HGIS de las Indias for Spanish America | Year of event | None | historical-basemaps gap 1815 to 1878 |

The order of work follows from this. First, pull the 2,042 P11085 items and their fate properties from Wikidata on an unrestricted machine, then measure how many Spanish items have a capture event, "destroyed by" or coordinates. Second, parse the Spanish ships of the line list and the Todo a Babor list into fate rows, and join each to "List of naval battles" on battle name and date. Third, geocode battle locations, preferring Wikidata battle coordinates where they exist, and fall back to the CLIWOC noon position of a participating ship on the action date. Fourth, append SlaveVoyages captures, whose port codes give origin and intended destination. Fifth, write to Cy Harrison for permission to extract Three Decks events, which would fill the years before 1796 that Todo a Babor's list does not cover and would add captor names. Wreck datasets add little here: they rarely carry flag or cause, so they only contribute where a keyword classifier finds terms such as "captured" or "burnt" in history text.

Two gaps remain at the end of this plan. Origin and destination for captured warships do not exist as fields in any source found; Three Decks events give only the places a ship passed through. And merchant captures before 1793 rest on Prize Papers material whose digitized extent and export could not be confirmed. Any count of "how many Spanish ships Britain took" will undercount merchant prizes until that portal can be tested directly.

## Conclusion

The barrier to the Royal Navy map is not missing history but missing geometry and missing permission. The event structure already exists in Three Decks, the capture codes in SlaveVoyages, and the prize records with the University of Oldenburg and The National Archives. None of them stores a coordinate, and the richest of them reserves all rights. The project's first deliverable should therefore be a geocoded battle and port gazetteer, not a ship list. Once each named battle and port has a point, every other source attaches to it by name and date.

The single largest unknown is cheap to resolve: one unrestricted SPARQL run against the 2,042 P11085 items will show whether Wikidata already holds enough capture events and battle coordinates to cover the naval side. If it does, the warship layer can be built without touching Three Decks. If it does not, the decision point becomes Cy Harrison's answer about Three Decks, and the merchant side waits on whether the Prize Papers portal offers an export.
